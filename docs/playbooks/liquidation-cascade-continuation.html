Hypothesis

When forced liquidations, open-interest contraction, directional price movement, and depleted depth occur together, continuation may persist briefly before liquidity replenishes, net of unusually high costs.

Entry conditions

Long

  • Short liquidations exceed the frozen threshold while price rises and open interest contracts.
  • Aggressive buy flow remains dominant after the first burst and executable depth is measurable.
  • Index/oracle inputs are healthy and the caller's maximum cost constraint is satisfied.
  • The signal is ignored if the event is already older than the tested latency budget.

Short

  • Long liquidations exceed the frozen threshold while price falls and open interest contracts.
  • Aggressive sell flow remains dominant after the first burst and executable depth is measurable.
  • Index/oracle inputs are healthy and the caller's maximum cost constraint is satisfied.
  • The signal is ignored if the event is already older than the tested latency budget.

Invalidation

  • Liquidation flow and directional imbalance stop while depth replenishes.
  • Price reclaims the predeclared event-origin level against the position.
  • Oracle, index, venue, or data-feed health becomes uncertain.

Exit logic

  • Exit when liquidation intensity normalizes, imbalance reverses, or the short maximum holding window expires.
  • Use a hard structural stop with conservative gap and slippage assumptions.
  • Do not assume a take-profit fill inside an unavailable or crossed book.

Cost model

Use event-time depth and a latency-sensitive impact model; stress costs beyond historical observations because the setup operates in impaired markets.

  • maker-taker-fees
  • spread
  • slippage
  • market-impact
  • funding

Risk constraints

  • Size from a caller-supplied risk budget but apply an additional executable-depth cap.
  • Use lower leverage and a larger liquidation buffer than in normal liquidity.
  • Reject the trade if expected impact or data latency cannot be bounded.

Failure modes

  • The continuation window may be shorter than observable data and order latency.
  • Extreme spread and impact can erase the gross move or make exit impossible.
  • Liquidation feeds can be incomplete, delayed, duplicated, or venue-specific.
  • A cascade can reverse violently once forced flow is exhausted.

Validation plan

  • Use event-time simulation and reject bar-only tests that cannot establish order sequence.
  • Audit feed latency, missing events, duplicate events, and venue outages before measuring returns.
  • Stress spread, impact, latency, and partial-fill assumptions well beyond their median values.
  • Report tail loss, fill probability, time-to-fill, after-cost expectancy, and results by event severity.
