Entry conditions

Long

  • The frozen compression measure is below its tested historical quantile before the break.
  • A closed 15m bar exceeds the predeclared upper range boundary by the tested confirmation distance.
  • Range and volume expand without a liquidity dislocation or stale print.
  • The context does not show an established higher-timeframe downtrend unless that variant is tested separately.

Short

  • The frozen compression measure is below its tested historical quantile before the break.
  • A closed 15m bar falls below the predeclared lower range boundary by the tested confirmation distance.
  • Range and volume expand without a liquidity dislocation or stale print.
  • The context does not show an established higher-timeframe uptrend unless that variant is tested separately.

Invalidation

  • Price closes back inside the broken range under the frozen false-break rule.
  • Expansion is caused by an unhealthy index/oracle or venue-specific data error.
  • Spread, impact, or slippage exceeds the caller's maximum execution constraint.

Exit logic

  • Use a predeclared ATR trailing stop or opposite range boundary; compare variants without final-period selection.
  • Apply a time exit if volatility expansion fails to persist.
  • Simulate stop gaps, partial fills, fees, impact, and funding.

Cost model

Breakout entries are latency- and gap-sensitive; use conservative next-event fills and prohibit fills at a crossed historical trigger with no available depth.

Risk constraints

  • Determine size from the caller's risk budget, ATR stop distance, leverage cap, and executable depth.
  • Do not increase size because recent volatility is compressed without enforcing a shock cap.
  • Reject signals with insufficient margin headroom for an adverse gap.

Failure modes

  • Compression thresholds are sensitive to lookback, sampling, and structural volatility change.
  • False breaks are common in ranges and around engineered liquidity sweeps.
  • Volatility targeting can oversize exposure immediately before a shock.
  • Bar data can create impossible trigger, entry, and stop ordering.

Validation plan

  • Freeze compression and boundary rules, then use chronological walk-forward evaluation.
  • Run event-aware execution simulation and same-bar ambiguity stress tests.
  • Report parameter surfaces, not only the best point, across venues, directions, and regime states.
  • Evaluate after-cost expectancy, drawdown, tail loss, trade count, and stability on untouched data.
