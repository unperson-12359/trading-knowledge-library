    Island reversal reviewed #

    A classical reversal pattern in which a cluster of one or more trading sessions is isolated from the surrounding price action by a gap on each side — typically an exhaustion gap into the cluster and a breakaway gap out of it in the opposite direction. The stranded 'island' marks an abrupt sentiment flip, with island tops (bearish) more common than island bottoms. It is a technical-analysis hypothesis, not a proven predictive law.
    Intuition: Price leaps one way, trades in a brief frenzy, then leaps back the other way, leaving the late entrants marooned on an island of prices the market immediately abandoned.
    Mechanics: Require a lengthy prior trend, an initial gap (often exhaustion), a compact cluster of sessions trading in a definable range on increased volume, and a final gap in the opposite direction that leaves the cluster isolated. The two gaps are expected not to be filled for some time; the pattern is actionable once the second gap appears. No standard measured target exists.
    Failure modes: True islands are rare, and researchers note the pattern often yields poor performance results; many apparent islands get their second gap filled within days, voiding the isolation. In thin or illiquid markets, routine overnight gaps manufacture meaningless 'islands' constantly.
    Misconceptions: That all gaps eventually fill quickly — the island reversal is premised on the two flanking gaps staying open; also that a single-day island is required, whereas clusters can span days to weeks.
    Example: After a long rally a stock gaps up from $48 to $52, churns between $51 and $53 for five days on heavy volume, then gaps down to $47.50 — stranding the $51–$53 cluster as an island top that precedes a decline.
    Aliases: Island top, Island bottom, Island cluster
