    Market-impact model #

    A model of price movement caused by the strategy's own trading, often separated into temporary and persistent components.
    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It is part of the machinery that converts strategy instructions into simulated positions, cash flows, and performance.
    Mechanics: Estimate impact from participation, order size, volatility, liquidity, execution time, and calibrated coefficients; feed it into simulated fills and later prices if modeled.
    Failure modes: Simplified timing, liquidity, fee, borrow, funding, or corporate-action assumptions can make simulated results unattainable live.
    Misconceptions: A detailed simulator reduces modeling error but cannot reproduce every queue event or future market condition.
    Example: A 10% participation order receives more impact than a 1% participation order in the same stock and volatility regime.

    Borrow-cost model #

    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It is part of the machinery that converts strategy instructions into simulated positions, cash flows, and performance.
    Mechanics: For each short position, apply the dated borrow rate and availability to market value over elapsed time, accrue the charge, and reject or recall unavailable borrow when modeled.
    Failure modes: Simplified timing, liquidity, fee, borrow, funding, or corporate-action assumptions can make simulated results unattainable live.
    Misconceptions: A detailed simulator reduces modeling error but cannot reproduce every queue event or future market condition.
    Example: Borrowing $100,000 of stock for 30 days at 6% annualized accrues about $493 on an actual/365 basis.

    Corporate-action adjustment #

    The point-in-time treatment of splits, dividends, mergers, spinoffs, and other issuer events in prices, positions, orders, and cash flows.
    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It controls what information the model receives and in what numerical representation.
    Mechanics: On the event's effective timestamp, adjust quantities and reference prices or book the economic distribution without using later-known terms early.
    Failure modes: Fitting the operation on the full dataset leaks future information; revisions, outliers, and missing values can also distort results.
    Misconceptions: A cleaner or rescaled dataset is not automatically more truthful; transformations must preserve timing and economic meaning.
    Example: A 2-for-1 split changes 100 shares at a $60 basis into 200 shares at a $30 per-share basis without creating profit.

    Point-in-time data #

    Historical data stored as it was known or effective at each past decision time, including vintage dates and later revisions.
    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It controls what information the model receives and in what numerical representation.
    Mechanics: For every value and universe membership, select the version whose release timestamp was no later than the simulated decision timestamp.
    Failure modes: Fitting the operation on the full dataset leaks future information; revisions, outliers, and missing values can also distort results.
    Misconceptions: A cleaner or rescaled dataset is not automatically more truthful; transformations must preserve timing and economic meaning.
    Example: A March backtest uses the GDP estimate released in February, not the revised value published in June.

    Feature selection #

    Choosing a subset of candidate inputs using prespecified statistical, model-based, or domain criteria.
    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It estimates how a fitted choice behaves on observations that were not used to fit that choice.
    Mechanics: Fit the selector inside each training fold, apply it to that fold's validation data, and repeat selection when the training window changes.
    Failure modes: Random splitting, overlapping labels, repeated tuning, or an unprotected final test set can produce optimistic estimates.
    Misconceptions: Validation measures conditional generalization under a split design; it does not guarantee future market performance.
    Example: Within each fold, the 10 highest training-set mutual-information features are selected from 200 candidates.

    Cross-validation #

    A resampling procedure that repeatedly fits a model on one subset and evaluates it on held-out observations to estimate generalization and compare choices.
    Open unified concept + skill view →
    Intuition: It estimates how a fitted choice behaves on observations that were not used to fit that choice.
    Mechanics: Choose a splitter consistent with the data-generating process, fit the entire pipeline inside each training fold, score each held-out fold, and aggregate the scores.
    Failure modes: Random splitting, overlapping labels, repeated tuning, or an unprotected final test set can produce optimistic estimates.
    Misconceptions: Validation measures conditional generalization under a split design; it does not guarantee future market performance.
    Example: Five-fold cross-validation trains on four folds and scores the fifth five times, so every row is evaluated out of fold once.
