    Area under ROC curve #

    The area beneath the ROC curve, equivalently the probability that a randomly selected positive receives a higher score than a randomly selected negative, with tie handling.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Compute the ROC curve across thresholds and numerically integrate true-positive rate with respect to false-positive rate.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: An AUC of 0.70 means a random positive outranks a random negative about 70% of the time under the metric's tie convention.
    AUC = integral of TPR with respect to FPR

    Calibration #

    Agreement between forecast probabilities and observed event frequencies, such that events assigned probability p occur about proportion p in comparable cases.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Bin or smooth held-out forecasts, compare mean predicted probability with empirical frequency, and assess with reliability plots and proper scoring rules.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: Among 200 held-out cases forecast near 0.70, 136 occur, giving an observed rate of 0.68.

    Brier score #

    The mean squared error of probabilistic forecasts for binary outcomes, and more generally the summed squared probability error across classes under a stated convention.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Subtract each binary outcome from its forecast probability, square the error, and average over held-out cases.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: Forecasts 0.8 and 0.3 for outcomes 1 and 0 give Brier = (0.04 + 0.09)/2 = 0.065.
    Brier = mean((p - y)^2)

    Log loss #

    A proper scoring loss equal to the negative log probability assigned to the observed class, averaged across observations.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Clip probabilities away from zero for numerical safety, select the probability of each observed class, take negative logs, and average.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: A 0.8 forecast for an event that occurs incurs loss -log(0.8), about 0.223.
    Binary log loss = -mean(y log p + (1-y) log(1-p))

    Mean absolute error #

    The average absolute difference between continuous predictions and observed targets.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Compute each residual, take its absolute value, and average the values, optionally using prespecified observation weights.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: Errors of -2, 1, and 3 produce MAE = (2+1+3)/3 = 2.
    MAE = mean(abs(y - prediction))

    Root mean squared error #

    The square root of mean squared error, placing the error summary in the target variable's original units.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: Calculate held-out squared residuals, average them, and take the nonnegative square root.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: If MSE is 4.00 percentage-points squared, RMSE is 2.00 percentage points.
    RMSE = sqrt(mean((y - prediction)^2))

    Mean absolute percentage error #

    The average absolute forecast error divided by the absolute observed value, expressed as a ratio or percentage under a stated zero-value convention.
    Intuition: It summarizes one aspect of prediction error or decision quality in a number or curve.
    Mechanics: For nonzero targets, divide each absolute residual by the absolute target and average; explicitly handle or exclude zero and near-zero denominators.
    Failure modes: Class imbalance, threshold choice, unequal error costs, outliers, and dependence can make the summary inappropriate or unstable.
    Misconceptions: No single metric captures economic utility, turnover, risk, calibration, and trading costs at once.
    Example: Actual values 100 and 200 with forecasts 90 and 220 yield MAPE = (10%+10%)/2 = 10%.
    MAPE = mean(abs((y - prediction) / y))

    Benchmark strategy #

    A simple, investable, and explicitly specified reference strategy used to judge whether a more complex strategy adds economic value.
    Intuition: It turns an informal trading idea into a claim or design choice that can be evaluated against dated evidence.
    Mechanics: Choose the benchmark before testing, simulate it over identical assets, dates, constraints, costs, and risk conventions, then compare incremental results.
    Failure modes: Results can mislead when hindsight, multiple testing, nonstationarity, costs, or unavailable historical information are ignored.
    Misconceptions: A favorable historical result is evidence about the tested sample and assumptions, not proof of a durable profitable edge.
    Example: A stock-selection strategy is compared with a costed equal-weight portfolio rebalanced on the same monthly dates.

    Edge #

    A positive conditional expected economic advantage of a specified decision rule over its benchmark after realistic costs and risks, estimated with uncertainty.
    Intuition: It turns an informal trading idea into a claim or design choice that can be evaluated against dated evidence.
    Mechanics: Define payoff and benchmark, estimate expected net payoff on protected data, quantify sampling uncertainty and downside, and monitor prospective decay.
    Failure modes: Results can mislead when hindsight, multiple testing, nonstationarity, costs, or unavailable historical information are ignored.
    Misconceptions: A favorable historical result is evidence about the tested sample and assumptions, not proof of a durable profitable edge.
    Example: With 55% wins of $1, 45% losses of $1, and $0.03 cost per trade, estimated edge is $0.07 per trade before uncertainty.
    Expected net edge = probability of win times average win - probability of loss times average loss - costs
