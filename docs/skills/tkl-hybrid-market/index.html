    Concept evidence

    A market structure combining elements of auction/order-driven trading and dealer/quote-driven trading — typically an electronic limit order book operating alongside designated market makers or a floor auction with assigned liquidity obligations.

    Intuition

    Part robot, part human: most orders match electronically, but assigned market makers still quote and can step in when the book is thin.

    Mechanics

    A central order book provides automated execution, while registered market makers quote within it (and may receive obligations and privileges); some venues also retain a floor or single-price auction for the open, close, and volatile periods. The NYSE Hybrid Market, approved by the SEC in 2006, increased automated executions alongside its floor auction.

    Failure modes

    The two mechanisms can conflict in stress: market maker obligations may be suspended exactly when needed, and interaction between automated quotes and human crowd interest can produce confusing or dual liquidity pools.

    Misconceptions

    Hybrid does not mean half-manual in a nostalgic sense — modern hybrids are overwhelmingly electronic, with human or obligated elements layered on top for specific functions like the opening auction.

    Example

    NYSE combines an electronic limit order book with Designated Market Makers who have quoting obligations and who facilitate the opening and closing auctions on the floor.

    Citations
