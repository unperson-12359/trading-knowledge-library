Order-flow imbalance

Explain, compare, or apply Order-flow imbalance using the library's structured Microstructure reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

DomainMicrostructure
Concept IDmicrostructure/order-flow-imbalance

Trading concept

The net excess of demand over supply at the best bid and ask, defined by Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov (2014) from all order book events - limit orders, market orders, and cancellations - at the best prices. They show short-horizon price changes are largely driven by this imbalance, with an approximately linear relation whose slope is inversely proportional to market depth.

Intuition

Prices move on the net pressure of orders arriving and leaving the inside market, not on raw trade counts.

Mechanics

Each order book event contributes positively when it adds bid-side or removes ask-side quantity and negatively otherwise; the cumulative imbalance over an interval is then related to the contemporaneous mid-price change. Unlike signed trade volume, OFI incorporates quote updates and cancellations.

Formula
OFI = sum over events e_n, with e_n = +Dq_b for bid-side quantity increases, -Dq_b for bid-side decreases, and symmetric negative contributions for ask-side events (Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov convention).

Failure modes

The linear relation is a short-horizon statistical regularity that breaks down around news jumps and requires full order-event data; estimates degrade when depth is manipulated or rapidly cancelled.

Misconceptions

Order-flow imbalance is not just signed trade volume: limit-order submissions and cancellations at the inside move it as much as executed trades do.

Example

Over one second, 3,000 net shares are added at the best bid while the ask queue thins; the mid-price typically ticks up by an amount inversely proportional to book depth.
