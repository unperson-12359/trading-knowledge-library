    Cash market

    Explain, compare, or apply Cash market using the library's structured Instruments reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

    DomainInstruments
    Concept IDinstruments/cash-market

    Trading concept

    The market for the physical or cash commodity (or asset) itself, as contrasted with futures or other derivative contracts on it. It may take the form of an organized central market, a decentralized over-the-counter market, or a local organization serving a small region, and trades settle with prompt delivery and payment through customary market channels.

    Intuition

    The cash market is where the actual thing changes hands now, not a promise about the future. Its price is the reference point to which futures and forward prices are compared.

    Mechanics

    Buyer pays seller and delivery occurs within the market's standard settlement period (immediate for spot commodities, T+1/T+2 for most securities). Cash prices are often quoted relative to futures as a basis (e.g., cents over or under a futures month), and cash and futures prices tend to converge as the delivery month approaches.

    Failure modes

    Cash prices can be thin, local, or grade-specific, so they may not match the standardized grade underlying a futures contract, creating basis risk. Storage, transport, and delivery frictions can also keep cash and futures prices apart longer than convergence arguments suggest.

    Misconceptions

    That the cash market is a single centralized exchange; for many commodities it is a decentralized web of dealers, elevators, and processors. Also, 'cash' does not mean payment in banknotes — it contrasts with 'futures', not with credit.

    Example

    A grain elevator buys corn from a farmer at the local cash price quoted as 10 cents under December futures; the farmer delivers physical corn and is paid promptly.
