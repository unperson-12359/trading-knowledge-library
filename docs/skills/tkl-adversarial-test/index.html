    Adversarial test

    Explain, compare, or apply Adversarial test using the library's structured AI and knowledge engineering reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

    adversarial testingred teamingattack simulation
    Skilltkl-adversarial-test
    DomainAI and knowledge engineering
    Concept IDai-and-knowledge-engineering/adversarial-test

    Trading concept

    Deliberately probing an AI system with inputs designed to make it fail — evasion attacks, poisoned data, prompt injections, extraction attempts — in order to find vulnerabilities before an adversary does. NIST's adversarial machine learning taxonomy (NIST AI 100-2) classifies such attacks by ML method, lifecycle stage, and attacker goals, capabilities, and knowledge, and pairs them with mitigations.

    Intuition

    Hire the burglar to test the locks: instead of asking 'does it work?', ask 'how can it be made to break?'

    Mechanics

    Testers enumerate the attack surface (inputs, retrieved content, tools, training data) and mount attack classes from the taxonomy — e.g., crafting inputs that bypass safety controls or manipulate decisions — then record success rates and trace them to mitigations such as input filtering, least privilege, and human approval for high-risk actions. Penetration-testing practice treats the model as an untrusted user.

    Failure modes

    A passed adversarial test proves only that the tested attacks failed — novel attack classes and adaptive attackers evolve past any fixed test suite. Tests scoped to the model alone miss system-level paths (tools, retrieval corpora, downstream consumers of output).

    Misconceptions

    Adversarial testing is not the same as accuracy testing on hard examples; it assumes an intelligent opponent who adapts. Red teaming once, pre-launch, is not a durable defense — it must recur as the system and attack landscape change.

    Example

    Before enabling a trading assistant's report-summarization feature, testers feed it research PDFs containing hidden injected instructions and verify that approval gates and output filters block every attempted policy override.

    Citations

    • NIST AI 100-2 E2025, 'Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations' — Publication abstract (NIST CSRC)
