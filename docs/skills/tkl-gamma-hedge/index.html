Gamma hedge

Trading concept

An options-hedging technique that neutralizes a position's gamma - the rate at which delta changes per $1 move in the underlying - so that the delta hedge remains effective for larger price moves without constant rebalancing.

Intuition

Gamma is how fast your delta hedge goes stale; hedging gamma keeps the hedge from drifting out of alignment every time the underlying moves.

Mechanics

Long options (calls or puts) have positive gamma, short options negative gamma, and the underlying itself has zero gamma, so gamma can only be hedged by adding other options: the trader first sets total gamma to zero with a traded option, then restores delta neutrality with the underlying. Gamma is highest for at-the-money options near expiration.

Formula
Position gamma = sum over positions of (quantity * multiplier * gamma_i); choose an options position Q such that position gamma + Q * multiplier * gamma_hedge_instrument = 0, then re-neutralize delta.

Failure modes

Gamma hedges are themselves unstable near expiration, when ATM gamma spikes, and require trading additional options with their own costs and vega exposure; a gamma-neutral book still carries higher-order and volatility risk.

Misconceptions

You cannot gamma-hedge with the underlying stock - stock positions have no gamma because their delta is fixed at 1 or -1; only options change a position's gamma.

Example

A market maker short a 0.50-delta call with gamma 0.06 buys enough of another listed option with gamma 0.03 to bring book gamma to zero, then adjusts shares to restore delta neutrality.
