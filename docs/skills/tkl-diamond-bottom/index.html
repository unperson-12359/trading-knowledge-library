Diamond bottom

Diamond bottom reversal
Skill packagetkl-diamond-bottom
DomainChart patterns
Concept IDchart-patterns/diamond-bottom

Trading concept

A rare classical reversal pattern at the end of a downtrend whose outline resembles a diamond, formed by an initial broadening phase (higher peaks and lower valleys) followed by a contracting phase (lower peaks and higher valleys). The conventional expectation is an upward breakout reversing the prior decline; in Bulkowski's sample, diamond bottoms with upward breakouts were the best-performing of the 36 patterns he ranked for downward breakouts. It is a technical-analysis hypothesis, not a proven predictive law.

Intuition

Panic first whips price violently in both directions, then the swings die down into a point — the diamond marks the moment the selling storm blows itself out and buyers quietly take over.

Mechanics

Draw trend lines connecting peaks and valleys: diverging in the first half, converging in the second, with price touching each boundary once or twice; volume trends downward about 67% of the time. Confirmation is a close outside a boundary, which historically is upward about 74% of the time. The measure rule projects the pattern's height from the breakout; after a quick plunge into the diamond, price often recovers toward (but usually just short of) the launch level.

Formula
Price target = B +/- (P - V), where B = breakout price, P = highest peak in the diamond, and V = lowest valley (sign follows breakout direction).

Failure modes

The shape is genuinely difficult to spot — skewed diamonds and price outliers cause frequent misidentification — and throwbacks/pullbacks after the breakout hurt performance. The remaining ~26% of downward resolutions can be severe continuations of the downtrend.

Misconceptions

That every diamond-shaped wiggle is tradeable — without the clear broadening-then-contracting structure and a confirmed close outside the boundary, the 'pattern' is noise.

Example

After a fast drop from $50 to $38, a stock thrashes between $36 and $44 with widening then narrowing swings; a close above the upper-right boundary near $42 confirms the diamond bottom, and price recovers toward the $48–$50 launch area.
