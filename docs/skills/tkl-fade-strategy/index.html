Fade strategy

Trading concept

A contrarian tactic that trades against the prevailing move - selling into strength or buying into weakness - on the premise that the late stages of a move are driven by slow-reacting traders and prone to reversal. Common applications include fading overextended intraday moves and fading the initial reaction to economic data releases.

Intuition

If everyone who wanted to buy has already bought, the marginal flow runs out and the move stalls; fading is selling the exhaustion of a crowded move rather than its beginning.

Mechanics

Identify an extended move (a news spike, vertical run, or stretched deviation from a reference), enter against it with a defined invalidation beyond the extreme, and target reversion toward the pre-move level or a mean. Fading economic releases typically waits for the first reaction to complete before entering against it, with wider stops to survive post-release volatility.

Failure modes

Trends persist far beyond apparent exhaustion, so fading strong trends produces repeated losses punctuated by occasional large ones when the trader averages into the fade; on regime-changing news the reversal simply never comes.

Misconceptions

That fading is synonymous with recklessness; disciplined fades use the same invalidation-first construction as any other setup, but it is a regime-dependent framework rather than a validated general edge.

Example

After a payrolls beat sends USD/JPY up 80 pips in minutes, a fade trader sells the pair once momentum stalls, targeting a retrace of half the spike with a stop above the reaction high.
