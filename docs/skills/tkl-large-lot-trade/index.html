    Large-lot trade

    Explain, compare, or apply Large-lot trade using the library's structured Volume and order flow reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

    DomainVolume and order flow

    Trading concept

    A single executed trade (or an exchange-fragmented series of prints from one large order) whose size is far above the typical trade size for that instrument, taken in order-flow practice as a sign of institutional participation. Platforms surface them with filters that flag prints above absolute or adaptive size thresholds.

    Intuition

    Retail trades in ones and tens; when a 2,000-lot print hits the tape, someone with serious size is doing business.

    Mechanics

    Detected by scanning the time-and-sales stream for trades exceeding a fixed or adaptive threshold (platforms such as ATAS re-aggregate the fragmented prints exchange matching algorithms produce and mark the consolidated size on the chart).

    Failure modes

    Large prints include spread rolls, block reporting, and opening/closing auction volume, which carry different information than directional institutional aggression; size thresholds that work in one volatility regime mislead in another.

    Misconceptions

    A large print is not automatically 'smart money' to follow — the initiator may be liquidating, hedging, or rolling, and the print says nothing about their remaining intent.

    Example

    On a day when the median ES trade is 3 contracts, the tape prints a single 1,800-lot at the offer near a session high; the platform flags it and the trader watches whether price accepts above that level.
