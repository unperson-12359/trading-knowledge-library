    Last point of support

    Explain, compare, or apply Last point of support using the library's structured Wyckoff reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

    Trading concept

    The Wyckoff event (LPS) marking the low of a reaction or pullback after a sign of strength, typically a retreat to former resistance-turned-support on diminished spread and volume. It is regarded as a low-risk area to initiate or add to long positions before markup.

    Intuition

    After the breakout rally, the market takes one last quiet step back to the old ceiling, now a floor — the final boarding call for longs.

    Mechanics

    Once an SOS demonstrates demand, subsequent pullbacks are monitored: an LPS holds at or above prior resistance with narrowing spread and shrinking volume, indicating supply is exhausted. Despite the singular name, several LPSs can occur; Wyckoff's point-and-figure counts are conventionally anchored at the LPS level.

    Failure modes

    A pullback labeled LPS can fail and re-enter the trading range, invalidating the markup scenario; distinguishing a healthy LPS from the start of a reversal is only possible as subsequent action unfolds.

    Misconceptions

    The LPS is not necessarily the last pullback ever, nor must it touch the exact old resistance; it is a zone defined by diminished volume and spread, not a precise price.

    Example

    After jumping above its 102 range top, a stock pulls back to 103 on half the breakout volume; the trader buys there with a stop below the old range, treating the dip as the LPS.

    Citations

    • The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial (StockCharts.com ChartSchool, by Roman Bogomazov and Michael Lipsett) — Accumulation: Wyckoff Events (LPS); Wyckoff Point and Figure (P&F) Count Guide
