Factor exposure

Explain, compare, or apply Factor exposure using the library's structured Portfolio management reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

Skill packagetkl-factor-exposure
DomainPortfolio management
Concept IDportfolio-management/factor-exposure

Trading concept

The sensitivity of an asset's or portfolio's return to a given factor - the change in return per unit change in the factor - represented by the coefficient b_ij multiplying that factor in a linear factor model.

Intuition

It is a dial reading showing how strongly an asset or portfolio is tuned to a driver like the market, size, or value: exposure 1.2 to the market factor means roughly 1.2x the factor's moves.

Mechanics

Asset exposures b_ij are estimated by regression or specified from characteristics; a portfolio's exposure to factor j is the weighted average of its assets' exposures, b_pj = sum_i w_i * b_ij, i.e., b_p = x'B in matrix form. Exposures map factor returns into asset returns and factor covariances into the asset covariance matrix.

Formula
r_i = sum_j b_ij * f_j + e_i, where b_ij is the exposure of asset i to factor j; portfolio exposure b_p = x'B.

Failure modes

Exposures are estimated, not observed: they drift over time as companies and funds change, differ across data windows and estimators, and characteristic-based proxies (e.g., book-to-market for value) only approximate true loadings.

Misconceptions

A fund labeled 'value' does not necessarily have high value-factor exposure - exposure must be measured from return behavior or holdings, not inferred from the label.

Example

A portfolio of 70% in a stock with market exposure 1.2 and 30% in a bond fund with market exposure 0.1 has portfolio market exposure 0.7*1.2 + 0.3*0.1 = 0.87.
