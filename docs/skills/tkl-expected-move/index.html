Trading concept

The expected move is the estimated price range within which an underlying is expected to trade by a given expiration with approximately one-standard-deviation (~68%) probability, derived from option prices — typically computed as about 85% of the at-the-money straddle price or directly from ATM implied volatility scaled by the square root of time.

Intuition

It is the options market's plus-or-minus error bar on the stock: a $100 stock with a $5 expected move is priced to stay roughly between $95 and $105 two-thirds of the time.

Mechanics

Vendor conventions vary: Barchart computes the expected move as 0.85 x the weighted average of the two calls and two puts nearest the money; brokerage platforms often display S x IV x sqrt(T/365). The expected move is used to place strikes for credit spreads and condors outside the priced range and to contextualize event risk.

Formula
Expected move ~ 0.85 x ATM straddle, or S x IV x sqrt(T/365) for a 1-standard-deviation range over T days.

Failure modes

The 68% interpretation assumes a distribution the market does not actually price: fat tails make out-of-range outcomes far more frequent than one-sigma intuition suggests, and event windows concentrate the move into minutes, so the range is routinely breached around catalysts.

Misconceptions

The expected move is not a target or support/resistance level with predictive force — it is a restatement of option prices. Selling strikes 'just outside the expected move' is selling roughly one-sigma insurance, with the tail risk that implies, not a statistical edge.

Example

XYZ at $200 with 30-day IV of 24%: the one-month expected move is about 200 x 0.24 x sqrt(30/365) ≈ $13.75, so the platform displays an expected range of roughly $186 to $214.
