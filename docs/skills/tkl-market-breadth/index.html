Market breadth

Concept IDmicrostructure/market-breadth

Trading concept

One of the classical dimensions of market liquidity identified by Sarr and Lybek (IMF, 2002): the presence of large-volume orders such that sizable trades can be executed with only a negligible effect on prices. In microstructure it denotes order-size capacity, distinct from the technical-analysis usage (advancers versus decliners).

Intuition

It answers: how wide is the river - can a whale trade without making a splash?

Mechanics

Assessed through the size of orders quoted at and behind the best prices, volume and turnover statistics, and price-impact-per-volume measures; broad markets absorb large orders near current quotes.

Failure modes

Breadth is state-dependent and can evaporate in stress when large resting orders are cancelled; displayed size overstates true capacity because of cancellations and hidden-interest dynamics.

Misconceptions

As a liquidity dimension, breadth is unrelated to advance/decline 'market breadth' indicators used in technical analysis; the shared name is a collision of terminologies.

Example

A market in which a 50,000-share order executes within a cent of the midpoint without exhausting the inside quotes is broad.
