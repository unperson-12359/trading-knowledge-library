Kicker pattern

Trading concept

A two-candlestick reversal pattern consisting of two opposite-colored marubozu-type candles: the first moves with the prevailing trend, and the second gaps open at or beyond the first candle's open and drives in the opposite direction, nullifying it. Technical analysts regard it as one of the strongest reversal formations, typically attributed to a sudden news-driven shift in sentiment.

Intuition

The market is moving firmly one way, then overnight something changes so completely that price gaps to the other side of the prior open and runs the opposite way all session, kicking the trend into reverse.

Mechanics

Bullish kicker: black candle (close near its low) in a downtrend, then a white candle that gaps up to open at or above the first candle's open and closes near its high, with no overlap of the two real bodies. Bearish kicker mirrors this. The gap plus zero body overlap is the defining feature; the gap is usually driven by a news event.

Formula
Bullish kicker when C1 < O1, O2 >= O1, C2 > O2, and the bodies do not overlap; bearish kicker mirrors with C1 > O1, O2 <= O1, C2 < O2.

Failure modes

In markets that rarely gap (spot forex, 24-hour crypto) genuine kickers almost never occur. Even in equities, a news-driven gap can be fully faded in the following sessions, so the 'strongest reversal' reputation does not make it reliable without context.

Misconceptions

It is not an engulfing pattern: the second candle opens beyond the first candle's open via a gap, rather than engulfing the body from within. The two candles also conventionally show little or no shadows (marubozu-like).

Example

A stock slides, closing near its low at 32.00 after opening at 34.00. Overnight, positive earnings news gaps it up to open at 34.10, and it rallies to close near its high at 36.50. The bullish kicker is read as an abrupt sentiment reversal.
