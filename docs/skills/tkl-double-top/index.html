Double top

Trading concept

A classical bearish reversal pattern in which price in an established uptrend forms two peaks at roughly the same level separated by a moderate trough, and the pattern completes only when price breaks below the intervening trough (support). It is interpreted as two failed attempts to overcome the same supply zone. It is a technical-analysis hypothesis about trend change, not a proven predictive law.

Intuition

The market tests the same high twice and fails both times, showing that sellers consistently overwhelm buyers at that level; breaking the low between the peaks confirms the sellers' victory.

Mechanics

Require a prior uptrend of months, two peaks within roughly 3% of each other separated by at least about a month, and an intervening decline of roughly 10–20%. Confirmation is a decisive close below the trough low, preferably on expanding volume; broken support often turns into resistance on a retest. The conventional target subtracts the peak-to-trough height from the support break.

Formula
Price target = S - (P - T), where P = peak price, T = intervening trough price, and S = support (trough) level at the break.

Failure modes

The most common failure is anticipating the pattern: until the trough support breaks, the two peaks are just resistance in an ongoing uptrend, and price frequently breaks to new highs instead. Peaks too close together (days, not weeks) usually represent ordinary resistance rather than a durable reversal.

Misconceptions

That two equal highs by themselves mark a top — the reversal is unconfirmed until support breaks. Also, a double top on a bar chart is unrelated to the bullish 'double top breakout' on point-and-figure charts.

Example

A stock rallies to $36.80, declines ~15% to a trough near $30.50, rallies back to $36.80, then falls and closes below $30.50 on rising volume — completing the double top with a target near $24.50.
