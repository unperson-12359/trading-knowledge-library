    Piercing line

    Explain, compare, or apply Piercing line using the library's structured Candlestick patterns reference, including failure modes and misconceptions.

    Piercing pattern
    Skilltkl-piercing-line
    DomainCandlestick patterns
    Concept IDcandlestick-patterns/piercing-line

    Trading concept

    A two-candlestick pattern in a downtrend: a long black candle is followed by a white candle that opens below the prior low (ideally with a gap) and closes more than halfway up into the prior black body. Technical analysts read it as a potential bottom reversal signal, a weaker cousin of the bullish engulfing pattern.

    Intuition

    Sellers gapped the market down at the open, but buyers drove price back up through more than half of the previous session's losses, piercing deep into their territory.

    Mechanics

    From OHLC data: candle 1 is a long black body; candle 2 opens at or below L1 and closes above the midpoint (O1 + C1) / 2 of the first body but below O1 (otherwise it would be a bullish engulfing). The deeper the close into the first body, the stronger the conventional reading.

    Formula
    Piercing line when C1 < O1, O2 <= L1, and (O1 + C1)/2 < C2 < O1.

    Failure modes

    A close that only just passes the midpoint is weak and often fails, especially in strong downtrends. On 24-hour markets like forex where gaps are rare, the opening-gap requirement is often relaxed, which dilutes the pattern.

    Misconceptions

    Closing above the prior candle's high or open turns it into an engulfing pattern, not a piercing line; the close must stop within the first body. It is also not a confirmed reversal without follow-through.

    Example

    A stock falls, printing a candle from 44.00 open to 42.00 close (low 41.80). The next day opens at 41.50 and closes at 43.50, above the 43.00 midpoint of the prior body: a piercing line that bulls read as a bottoming attempt.
