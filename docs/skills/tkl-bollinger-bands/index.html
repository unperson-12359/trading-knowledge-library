Bollinger Bands

Trading concept

John Bollinger's volatility bands: a 20-period simple moving average flanked by upper and lower bands set k standard deviations (default 2) of price above and below it, so the envelope widens as volatility rises and contracts as it falls.

Intuition

The bands define 'relatively high' and 'relatively low' statistically: price at the upper band is high relative to recent volatility, at the lower band relatively low - without implying a reversal by itself.

Mechanics

Middle band = SMA(20); upper = middle + k x sigma(20), lower = middle - k x sigma(20), where sigma is the 20-period standard deviation of price (same window as the SMA). Bollinger suggests k = 2.1 for 50-period and 1.9 for 10-period settings. Used for band tags, M-top/W-bottom confirmation, and the Squeeze via BandWidth.

Formula
Middle = SMA(P, 20); Upper = Middle + k x StdDev(P, 20); Lower = Middle - k x StdDev(P, 20); k = 2 by default.

Failure modes

Band touches are 'tags', not signals: in strong trends price walks the band for long stretches, and the SMA base lags; squeezes flag volatility compression but give no direction, and post-squeeze breaks can be head-fakes.

Misconceptions

A tag of the upper band is not an automatic sell - 'relatively high' is not 'about to fall'; nor do the bands contain a normal distribution's 95% of prices, since returns are not normal (Bollinger cites 88-89% empirically).

Example

SMA(20) = 50 with sigma = 1.5: upper band = 53, lower = 47; a close at 53.4 is above the band - a tag, not a signal.
