Price-to-sales ratio

Concept IDmacro-and-fundamentals/price-to-sales-ratio

Trading concept

Market capitalization divided by trailing revenue - equivalently price per share over revenue per share - the amount investors pay per dollar of a company's sales. It remains usable when earnings are negative, which the P/E ratio is not.

Intuition

Sales are the hardest income-statement line to fake and exist even for loss-making firms, so P/S is the multiple of last resort for young or distressed companies.

Mechanics

Divide market capitalization by trailing-twelve-month revenue. Because the ratio ignores costs entirely, it must be read alongside margins: a firm at 1x sales with 20% net margins is effectively on a 5x P/E.

Formula
P/S = Market capitalization / Trailing 12-month revenue = Price per share / Revenue per share.

Failure modes

It treats a dollar of grocery revenue like a dollar of software revenue, so low-margin, high-revenue firms look deceptively cheap, and it ignores leverage - EV/Sales is the better tool for indebted firms.

Misconceptions

A low P/S does not imply undervaluation - thin-margin businesses rationally trade near zero - and the ratio says nothing about whether sales will ever convert to profit.

Example

A retailer with a $50 billion market cap and $100 billion of sales trades at 0.5x sales; with a 2% net margin that equates to a P/E of 25 - not obviously a bargain.
